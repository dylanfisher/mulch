# 0334 — A leaning noise comes round on a twisted lattice

- **Date:** 2026-09-10
- **Status:** accepted, landing the last two of
  [0331](0331-a-still-is-read-along-its-own-stops-per-pixel.md)'s four stills under
  [0332](0332-a-scene-is-the-colour-and-the-film-is-the-alpha.md)'s contract

**A sheared field cannot come round on the axes it was drawn on.** The tile is laid as a repeating
pattern, so every mark in a scene is snapped to meet itself at the join — a grating's period by
`sceneRepeat` and its lean by `sceneSlope`, both onto the mark's own period. That works because a
grating repeats every _period_: a lean of a whole period per tile depth is invisible at the join.
A noise repeats every _tile_, so the same snap leaves it a whole number of **cells** out of step
down the picture, and the only lean a plain wrap admits is one whole tile per tile — which is no
lean at all. The wind reaches a meadow and a canopy, so the wrap is what gives way.

**The wrap leans with the field.** `streakTiled` (src/lib/moireNoise.ts) hashes a cell corner on a
lattice generated by `(cols, 0)` and `(twist, rows)`, where `twist` is the whole number of cells the
snapped lean carries the field over the tile's depth: the x index has `twist` subtracted once per
whole tile of depth before it is wrapped. The field is then exactly periodic in both the tile's
width and its height with the shear included, at any lean a wind asks for, and it stays continuous
because the corner is a well-defined function of its own integer pair however it is reached. Every
scale snaps its own lean, because each has its own cell width; a lean that varies across the picture
— a standing gust — snaps per column, and comes round for the same reason.

**The scene is the colour and the ramp is what has to hold it.** Two stops are minted:
`--scene-canopy-shade`, because 0.38 turned out to be the lightness of a _lit_ leaf and a canopy
seen from under one is darker than any leaf ink this instrument held; and `--scene-meadow-tan`,
because a tan made by mixing the ember stop with the straw stop reads scarlet everywhere on its way
down — the fault three drawings of that still were tuned around (0331). **A stop a picture must
never reach is a stop its ramp must not hold**: the meadow's ramp no longer names `--screen-red`
and the canopy's no longer names `--drift-cool`, so the way down each of them passes through what
that field is actually made of. This is the second crossing of the colour boundary a landing still
has cost ([0236](0236-the-colour-boundary-is-a-gate-rule.md)), and the last: the four scenes have shipped.

**One snap, read two ways.** `sceneCells(across, period)` in src/lib/moireScene.ts is the count a
tile is divided into and `sceneRepeat` is `across / sceneCells(…)`: a mark cut by a cosine needs the
period and a mark placed by a hash needs the count, and two roundings of one quotient are two
chances to disagree about where a tile comes round. That identity is what makes `twist` land on a
whole number rather than a rounded one.

**A gust is a displacement and never a second lean.** A bake has no clock
([0126](0126-the-screen-rides-the-pictures-own-phase.md)), so the wind stands in the picture rather
than travelling through it. But the lean is snapped by `sceneSlope`, which is a **step function** of
what it is handed: a lean that varied across the picture tips that rounding column by column and
cuts a hard vertical break at every column where it tips — the same ruled grid the snapping exists
to prevent, moved off the tile join into the middle of the picture and standing in the same place in
every tile. So the lean is **one number for the tile** and the gust is a smooth offset on x,
periodic across the tile both ways and continuous everywhere. Anything a scene wants to vary across
a leaning field goes in the coordinate, never in the lean.

**And a fall to the foot is a band once a tile.** Both stills fell away toward the bottom of a
picture that never repeated; on a tile that is a bright line at every join. The fall is
`sceneAxis(y / height)` in both grounds instead — one tile deep, coming round at its own edges,
which is the perspective every ground has been under since
[0329](0329-a-yards-picture-is-a-scene-its-name-names.md).
