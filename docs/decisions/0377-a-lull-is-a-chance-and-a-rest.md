# 0377 — A lull is a chance and a rest, and the rest is the slot it rolls on

- **Date:** 2026-09-12
- **Status:** accepted, amending [0373](0373-a-lulls-lengths-are-seconds-on-one-dial-each.md),
  whose one-dial-in-seconds rule it keeps and whose ranges, gap and skip it drops.

**Three dials say what a lull does: how likely a rest is, how long one lasts, and how often the
odds are asked.** Eight dials — two ranges, a skip and a chance — were a run nobody could set on
purpose. The Chance is now rolled every Every seconds of playing, a hit holds the deck for exactly
one Rest, and the checks count again from the rest's end — so the deck plays a check's length before
the odds are asked again, and the top of the dial is a deck resting every check and never one held
for good. A rest of two and a check of a quarter is two seconds of silence after every quarter of
playing; both lengths are seconds on one logarithmic dial each, rounded onto the beat on the Grid. A resume lands exactly where the rest held it: the jump is
gone from the edge, the transport and the deck alike. The Grid is a two-step dial and not a picker,
the panner's way, and the Seed stands last.
