# 0313 — The session holds one ground yards may stand on

- **Date:** 2026-09-08
- **Status:** accepted, extending
  [0184](0184-the-ground-is-the-songs-and-a-part-plays-back-on-it.md) — the ground is the song's,
  and this is that claim one tier up — and resting on
  [0097](0097-yards-jump-on-one-session-clock.md), whose argument for a session field, for wall
  seconds and against a follower is borrowed whole.

**`Session.ground` is one crawl every yard with `bedTogether` on stands on.** A yard's own ground is
its own seed's, so two yards wander over unrelated parts of their sources and nothing brings them
together. The gesture a hand actually wants is that the loops move as one, and that is not a fact
about either yard: it is the second durable fact belonging to more than one deck, so it is the
session's, exactly as the jump clock is. `bedTogether` is per-yard because standing on it is —
which yards are together is a thing a hand sets one yard at a time.

**It is read at the instant a step begins, and by no other road.** The walk stays a pure function
of a yard's own seed and step count (0089, 0096), and the transport swaps the bed of the step it is
arming: `armStep` is the one place a step's own start time is known, and the shared ground is a
function of that instant. Two yards arming the same instant read the same offset without either
knowing the other exists — the same shape 0097 gives the jump clock, and the reason a shared render
is a function of the session rather than of the order its yards were pressed (0068). Together is to
the step: a landing reads the ground it opened on, so yards whose steps begin at different instants
are together within one landing, and the jump clock is what makes that exact.

**Its period is counted on one of two clocks: wall seconds, or one yard's arrangement.** Seconds
are what yards holding different loops can share — 0097's argument said one field along — and they
need nothing named: ticks are counted from the context's own zero and never from whichever yard was
played first.

**Parts and rounds need a yard named, and that is the one per-deck reference on the instrument.**
Parts belong to an arrangement and the session has none, so a ground counted in them counts _some
yard's_, and `ground.leader` says which. 0097 declined the transport follower because it made one
deck's transport a function of another's identity; the objections do not all carry here — a leader
follows nothing, so there are no cycles, and a removed leader has one obvious answer, which the
remove command writes: the leader is cleared and the ground holds still. What is true is that a led
ground is no longer a function of the clock alone, and the two consequences are stated rather than
hidden: **it moves only while the leader is playing**, and a yard may lead a ground it is not
standing on, because leading is a source of boundaries and nothing else.

**A leader with nothing arranged counts one whole row of its walk.** 0192's amendment said for
this ground rather than a second rule: a hand that pointed the shared ground at a yard asked for a
clock, and a yard with no parts entered still has the row the scope draws. The walk says where a row
turned over (`PlayerStep.rows`) and the leading voice reports it beside the two boundaries the
arrangement keeps, so both clocks fall back to the row and neither falls back to seconds — a period
of four on `parts` is still four somethings. It is the leader's own walk that is counted, whatever
that yard's own `bedPer` says: leading is a source of boundaries and nothing else, and a yard's
period is about where _its_ loop goes.

**The leader pushes; nobody re-derives anyone's walk.** The leading voice says so as it _arms_ a
step that opens a part or brings the arrangement round — before it reads its own bed, so the move
lands on the first jump of the part that caused it, exactly where a yard's own `bedPer: "part"`
ticks (0192). The host counts those boundaries into ticks and every yard on the ground reads that
count at the instant its own step begins. Followers arm inside the same lookahead, so they pick a
move up within one landing — the same guarantee Together already gives — and no follower pays for a
second copy of the leader's walk, which was the alternative and would have been invalidated by
every play, stop, seek and rate change the leader made. A render still reproduces: the tick count is
a function of arming, and the pump's order is fixed.

**One period, whatever it counts.** `every` is seconds on the wall clock and a whole count of parts
or rounds otherwise, bounded by the unit its own `per` names — 0192's "one period and not one per
unit" said for this ground. The press that changes the clock carries the reading that clock opens
at, because four seconds is not four parts.

**There is no bed it comes home to.** A yard's ground opens on the bed a hand put it on, and a
shared one cannot: two yards may hold two sources, so the bed one chose is nowhere on the other.
Offset zero — the loop itself, which `bedBounds` guarantees is always reachable — is the one ground
every yard has, so staying put comes home there.

**Its seed is a constant.** What varies a shared walk is its three words and its period, exactly as
they vary a yard's. A yard's crawl is drawn from the seed its whole pattern is and the session has
no such seed to spend, so a durable seed here would be a field with no gesture to turn it.

**While a yard is on it, everything of its own that authors a ground goes quiet.** Its period, the
bed its crawl came home to, the press that plants one and the grounds it kept: every one of them is
an author of where that loop is, and the shared ground already is one — two authors would be two
answers to that question (principle 1). The rows of words stay live and write the session's ground
instead, because they mean the same thing on either ground and only their owner differs; the
Counted in row stays too and becomes the shared ground's own clock, which is seconds and _another
yard's_ parts rather than this one's jumps.

**"Together" and not "Sync".** The header already carries that word for the shared jump clock, and
one word for two shared facts is the drift the copy modules exist to prevent (0021, 0097).
