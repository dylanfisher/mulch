# 0363 — A spark flashes a big mark, and an armed part writes one column

**2026-09-11.** `player.sparkPositions` reached only the waveform's cursors and `player.armed` only
the launch grid's ring. They reach the picture now, and neither costs the frame a draw: a spark is a
lift on the boxed read the landings and the output's two sides are already lifted on (0355, 0361),
and an armed part is one more field on the tile's key that the shop bakes off the frame (0354).

**A spark is a peak, and a peak is written as one big mark.** The whole ramp at the spark's own
level, over a `SPARK_CELLS` square of the boxed read at the column it reads — so the square goes to
the heaviest mark outright and walks back down the ramp as the flash falls. `SPARK_CELLS` is
`SCATTER_SPAN` (src/lib/moireScreenScatter.ts, 0352) and never a second choice of it: the scatter is
where this picture already decided how large a peak is drawn, and a mark five pixels a side is a
speck twice over (0345). The whole ramp and not one mark of it, which is what parts a flash from
the two lifts beside it — a landing's flare and a pan's lean are the picture standing a step denser
and stay on the ramp they read at, and a spark is an event that puts a mark there outright.

**A spark is an event and its position is not.** A spark's read walks the file while it sounds, so a
slot lit wherever its position moved would stand at full for as long as the spark lasted, which is a
flash held up rather than a flash. `MoireCellSpark.lit` is whether that slot was reading on the frame
before, slot for slot with `sparkPositions` — the peek reports a slot only once its own start has
passed and its sound is standing (0175), so the frame it appears on is the event. A slot that goes
quiet goes on falling where it stands: the end of a sound is not the end of its flash.

**It rides on the jolt, beside the pushes, and falls at their rate.** A flare and a flash are one
landing read twice, so `MoireJolt.sparks` sits beside `MoireJolt.pushes`, `carryJolt` carries both
across a rebuilt set, and the share of the loop they fall over is resolved once in `refillRows` and
spent on both (`CELL_DECAY`, 0355). `CELL_SPARKS` is `PLAYER_SPARK_COUNT_MAX` and not a ring of its
own size: how many companions a landing throws is already declared.

**The armed part is one cell column of the tile, and the hand or nothing.** `ScreenBake.armed` is an
alphabet name or null; where it is a name the tile's last cell column is baked in that hand and every
other column in the standing one, which is one compare a pixel and no second pass over the tile. It
reaches the key, so arming a part is one rebake and letting it go is another — off the frame, in the
shop's worker (0354). **A part queued into the hand already standing arrives null**: three alphabets
over six names, so about a third of the queues would otherwise bake a column in the hand it was
already in, which is a rebake that moves no pixel and an edge nobody could read. The comparison is
made where the read is taken (`armedAlphabet`, src/ui/moireRows.ts), because the one fact the bake is
asked for is whether what is coming is a _different_ hand.

**One column, and it is the tile's.** The tile is laid as a repeating pattern, so the picture's
rightmost cell column is that column and so is every one a tile width to the left of it: a queued part
reads as a rule of other marks recurring across the picture rather than as a single edge at the far
side. That is the price of announcing it without a second surface, and it is paid rather than
avoided — a picture-sized draw for one column is exactly what checkpoint A refused
(`STAMP_PICTURE_DRAWS`, 0353).

Neither half moves a picture-sized draw, adds a threshold pass or bakes anything on the frame: the
stamp still pays ten passes and one draw, and the armed hand is baked where every other term of the
tile is.
