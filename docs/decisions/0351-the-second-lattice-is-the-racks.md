# 0351 — The second lattice is the rack's

- **Date:** 2026-09-10
- **Status:** accepted, standing on [0278](0278-the-rack-shapes-the-picture.md),
  [0345](0345-the-picture-is-a-lattice-of-marks.md) and
  [0346](0346-the-lattice-stands-still.md), and landing the fourth of the eight the marks bench
  drew ([0347](0347-the-marks-bench-is-its-own-route.md))

The picture is one still lattice of marks, and one lattice beats against nothing. **A second
lattice of marks now stands over it wherever the rack is full**, in the same alphabet, on a cell of
its own — the bench's entry 02, landed bake-side in `src/ui/moireScreenBeat.ts`.

**The ratio is the two pitches the picture already has, and both are whole device pixels.** The
first lattice's cell is the screen's column pitch, five CSS pixels; the second's is its row pitch,
seven. Seven fifths, and never a dial: a cell at a hand-turned ratio is a bit laid at a fraction of
a pixel, which is the smear 0346 took out of the picture in the first place. Nothing new declares
the ratio — it is `gridPitchPx` against `rowPitchPx`, which is why it is already whole on every
display.

**The tile grows to the two cells' common multiple** (`screenTilePx`,
`src/ui/moireScreenTile.ts`): a tile is laid as a repeating pattern, so a width that does not come
round on a lattice steps that lattice by a fraction of a cell at every join, at full amplitude, once
a tile. How many beat cells that comes to is the two pitches' own business and never one number —
seven at every whole display ratio, eleven at three halves, one at four — and it is paid only while
the second lattice stands. At a fold of nought the width, the key and the tile are exactly the ones
0350 shipped, so a picture with an empty rack pays nothing for a lattice it does not draw.

Two things ride on the tile's width and moved with it. The three channels' own fringe cell is a beat
cell and is now read on its own stride rather than on the tile's, which were the same number until
this step. And the crawl sweeps the tile's period and comes back — a translation of exactly one tile
being the identity for a repeating pattern — so it sweeps the grown width, which makes it that many
times faster while the second lattice stands. The scenes snap their own periods to the width too, so
a full rack's ground is the same texture and not the same field as an empty rack's; §4 of the plan
holds that.

**Its presence is the rack's lattice fold and no look's** (0278). The reserved `lattice` look is the
one reading of how much rack is standing, and the cell it folds the field to has already travelled
over `SHAPE_SECS`; `latticeFold` reads that cell back as a turn, so the second lattice arrives on the
same travel the gutter thickens on. A rack of one entry is at the loosest cell, which is a fold of
nought: **the beat is the rack filling and never one effect**.

**Unioned into the alpha, never cut out of it.** Each lattice's mark is its own box read of the same
scene body at its own cell size — the same read, the same push, the same ten marks, with the
standing rack's own cell passes run over both (0348, 0349) — and the alpha a pixel carries is the
solider of the two coverages. Two lattices in one ink are one picture: a cell under both is no
solider than the solider of them, and neither takes ink from the other. Measured on the shipped bake
at two device pixels to the CSS one, the share of a tile's pixels carrying any ink goes from 0.71 to
0.83 when the rack fills.

**No second alphabet**, and none is wanted: what the coarse lattice says is where it stands, and it
says it in the marks the picture is already written in.

The cost is the bake. A tile seven times as wide is seven times the scene body, the cell grid
and the pixel loop, plus one more box read of that body for the coarse cells — and it is paid again
at each step of the fold's own ladder as the rack fills. It is paid only by a picture that is
drawing the second lattice, and never on a frame (0129).
